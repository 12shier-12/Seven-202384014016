# Triply — Two-Minute Demo Script

Open the app, keep this file next to you. Total: 2:00. Do not improvise: every step below is visible
in the UI without typing anything, because the seeded data already contains a conflict and a full
packing list.

## 0:00 — 0:15 | What it is

> "Triply is an offline-first trip planner. There is no backend and no account: everything lives in
> the browser's IndexedDB. It has two parts — a day-by-day itinerary, and a packing list generated by
> a rule engine."

Show the trip list: three trips, sorted by departure date, with upcoming / ongoing / past labels.

## 0:15 — 0:45 | Trips

1. Click **京都七日** (Kyoto, 7 days).
2. Point at the overview: 7 days, 22 activities, budget progress, packing progress.
3. Click **编辑行程**, change the party size from 2 to 3, save.

> "Trip data is the only input the packing engine needs: length, party size, tags and temperature."

## 0:45 — 1:15 | Itinerary and conflict detection

1. Open **按天时间线**.
2. Point at Day 2: Fushimi Inari 08:30 and the mountain hike 10:30 are marked amber.

> "The detector converts times to minutes and sweeps the day; it reports which two activities clash,
> not just that something is wrong."

3. Move an activity to another day with the **移到 Day N** menu; the daily subtotal updates.

## 1:15 — 1:50 | Packing list (the differentiator)

1. Open **打包清单**.
2. Point at the counter: "命中 6 条规则" and the progress bar.
3. Tick two items; change one quantity with **+**.
4. Open **设置**, disable **低温保暖**, go back to the list.

> "The list is derived, not merged: rules suggest, the user's edits are stored as overrides. Disabling
> a rule removes its suggestions but keeps my manual items and my edits — re-enabling restores them."

5. Re-enable the rule.

## 1:50 — 2:00 | Offline, export, close

1. Click **导出 CSV** (or **打印**), then show the downloaded file name.
2. Turn off Wi-Fi, reload the page: the app still opens and the ticked items are still ticked.

> "Installed as a PWA, precached by the service worker; all writes go to IndexedDB. The full source,
> tests and documentation are in the repository — 100 tests, all green."

## Backup lines (if asked)

- **Why no backend?** "Offline-first was a design choice: no server, no accounts, no privacy surface.
  The cost is single-device data, which JSON export/import handles."
- **Hardest part?** "Two things: making the packing list derived instead of merged, and writing a
  restricted expression parser so quantities can be `min(days, 7)` without `eval`."
- **What would you do next?** "Drag-and-drop reordering, custom rule editing, and an optional sync
  server — in that order."
